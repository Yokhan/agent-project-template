# Preserve Product Value

Use this semantic check when writing, shortening, or adapting customer-facing
copy that explains an offer. It supplements the selected writing mode; it does
not add a universal voice or replace the writing workflow.

Identify the supported customer situation, desired change, offer mechanism,
conditions, and next action from the current source of truth. Preserve the
customer-relevant advantage and the concrete situation it improves when
shortening; remove repetition and scaffolding first. A feature list or broad
benefit does not explain the value unless the reader can see what becomes
easier, possible, or less burdensome.

Do not invent exclusivity, competitor claims, outcomes, savings, testimonials,
bundled functionality, urgency, or terms. If the channel cannot fit an essential
distinction, compress it faithfully or surface the tradeoff. Before delivery,
check that every owner-emphasized advantage and its customer consequence remain
clear and that the copy still describes the same offer.

For example, if the supported advantage is that a replaceable exercise lets a
client keep following a plan when equipment or schedule changes, a short version
must preserve that consequence. Replacing it with “a personalized plan” removes
the useful distinction even if the shorter sentence sounds polished.
